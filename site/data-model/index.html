The shapes mission planning is built from

REMIT models a mission as a small set of immutable, attributed, content-addressed objects. This is a guided tour of those objects — what each one means, the rules they all obey, and how they fit together into a plan you can trust and replay.

📐 Derived from docs/remit-data-model.md (draft 0.3) and the Discussion Register. LinkML becomes the generating source once the schema is authored (DEC‑57).

§Three universal rules

Every object below obeys these three. They are why a REMIT plan can be compared, audited, and replayed perfectly years later.

🔒

Versioned objects are immutable

Requirements, baselines, excursions and profiles never mutate. A change is a new version with lineage back to the old one.

DEC-5DEC-7DEC-18
✍️

Everything decided is attributed

Every committing act carries a role-hat, an author, and a time. Nothing changes the record anonymously.

DEC-15NF2
🧬

Identity is content

An immutable object's ID is the hash of its canonical form. Names are just aliases; lineage is hash-linked and tamper-evident.

↻The lifecycle spine

The objects flow through one thin lap of the mission lifecycle (DEC-44). Each stage reads and writes a slice of the model.

Capture→ World→ Plan→ Compare→ Views→ Execute→ Learn

Capture turns intent into Requirements and Commitments · World assembles the Baseline and Excursions · Plan runs the kernel to produce Plans · Compare reads their scores under the comparability guard · Views project Entities across synchronised displays · Execute re-anchors the kernel to live State and appends to the log · Learn proposes new versions — never mutations.

1The requirement, and the promises in it

What the command wants, in their words — decomposed into individual promises the plan must keep.

Requirement

immutable · the primary object

The command's narrative intent, owned as a versioned object. Amend it and you get a new version with lineage — the original stands.

  • intentthe command's narrative, in their words
  • provenanceissuing role / authority + time
  • commitmentsthe promises it decomposes into
  • lineageprevious version · amending order ref

Commitment

a single promise

One thing the plan must achieve. Either hard (must hold) or soft with a priority. Carries the negotiated contract text and any unresolved ambiguities, and walks a lifecycle of states.

  • activitythe thing to do
  • criticalityhard · soft(priority)
  • captureanswers, echo-back text, open ambiguities
  • stateproposed → committed → satisfied / violated / waived
  • waiver?by / authority / when / why
DEC-16DEC-17DEC-22

Activity

the thing to actually do

The verb of a commitment — visit, loiter, avoid, transit, maintain. Where can be a waypoint or a boolean predicate over map cells; when is a window, before/after, or recurring.

  • typevisit · loiter · avoid · transit · maintain
  • wherewaypoint, or a predicate tree over cells
  • whenwindow · before · after · recurring
  • effectsself-state delta, persistent marker
  • relevant_channelswhat to sample / trigger on

2The world the plan moves through

The environment as known truth, plus the what-if overlays and edits applied on top of it. The kernel always evaluates baseline ⊕ excursion ⊕ plan-effects-to-date.

Baseline

immutable · one truth

The world as known: a medium (land / sea / air grid) carrying channels, and a single fact layer that observations append to.

  • mediumdomain, grid, cell attributes
  • channelsthe fields over space & time
  • factsthe one truth; observations append here
  • forecast_changepointswhen the world is expected to shift

Channel

a field over space & time

A quantity that varies across the map and the clock — tide, cloud, risk. Its values are a function of (cell, t), however realised.

  • valuesf(cell, t)
  • realisationraster · analytic · provider service
  • confidencehow trusted
  • freshnesshow recent
  • sampling_steptide coarse · cloud fine & wide

MovementModel

how costly it is to move

Per-domain cost of travel — and anisotropic: a property of the edge between cells, not the cell. Slope along direction on land; current / wind vectors at sea and in the air.

  • cost_speedf(cell→cell, t, profile)
  • realisationparametric(type, params) · provider

Excursion

immutable · a what-if overlay

A named, versioned set of deltas layered on a baseline — "what if the weather turns?" — without disturbing the underlying truth.

  • basethe baseline version it sits on
  • deltaa list of ChannelDeltas

ChannelDelta · Effect

edits to the world

How the world is changed. A delta is parametric (time-shift, scale, mask) or a raw cell patch. An Effect is a delta a plan's activity applies from a point in time.

  • ParametricDeltatype + params (registry)
  • CellPatch(cell, t, value) — the escape hatch
  • Effectsource, active_from, delta

3Own force

The platform doing the mission — its fixed capabilities, and its live condition.

Profile

immutable · capabilities

What a platform can do — the levers a sensitivity sweep varies.

The live condition the kernel re-anchors to during execution. It is the privileged self-entity — its fields are dynamic aspects (position, endurance).

4Entities & projections

Anything located with an identity — yourself, an actor, a feature, a phenomenon. Where a Channel is a field, an Entity is a thing, projected across synchronised views.

Entity

a located thing

Something with a place and a history. To affect the plan it is cast to a Channel or Commitment (an actor → a moving risk channel; a window → a timing commitment). In v1 entities are display-only.

  • kindself · actor · feature · phenomenon
  • provenanceplanned · forecast · observed
  • aspectsits time-varying facets
DEC-52

Aspect

one facet, over time

A single time-function of an entity — its position, an altitude, a timing window, an up/down status — rendered onto a shared axis by a view.

  • typecell · scalar · window · status
  • valuef(t) — a time function
  • unit? / channel_ref?unit · source channel

5Plan identity, and plans

A plan's identity is the fingerprint of everything that went into it — so two plans are only comparable when that fingerprint shares a basis.

Stamp

immutable · the fingerprint

Every input that determines a plan, bundled into one identity: which requirement, which world, which config core, what appetites and steering, which kernel and seed.

  • requirement_versionwhat was asked
  • baseline / excursionsthe world used
  • config_core_hashthe world-defining config
  • appetitesthe implementer's risk dials
  • steeringinterpreted gestures
  • kernel_version · seedpart of identity
DEC-23DEC-29DEC-48

Plan

immutable · a candidate answer

A candidate solution whose ID is the hash of its Stamp. Its materialisation (schedule, trajectory, state curves) is cached and regenerable; its scores and conflicts make it comparable.

  • id= hash(Stamp)
  • materialisationschedule · trajectory · state curves · verified
  • scoressatisfaction, cost band, robustness band
  • conflictsfirst-class, named clashes
DEC-5DEC-22DEC-29

Conflict

a named clash

When commitments cannot all be kept, the clash is a first-class object — named, with the parties and a narrative — not a silent failure.

  • kindstructural · emergent
  • partiesthe commitments in tension
  • narrativewhat the clash is, in words

6The record: why, and what happened

Decision and execution records, append-only. Together with stamps they give a complete after-action record with perfect replay.

SelectionRationale

why this plan

The record of a choice: which plan won, which it beat, on which axis, and who decided.

  • chosen / beatenthe winner and the field
  • deciding_axiswhat tipped it
  • by · role · atattribution
DEC-23

ExecutionLog

append-only · what happened

The live history of a mission: alerts when a band is crossed, observations that update truth, waivers, and replans — never overwritten.

  • Alerta band crossing (commitment / score / infeasible)
  • Observationa fact delta → the fact layer
  • Waivera relaxed commitment, attributed
  • Replanfrom one plan to another, with rationale

∞How it all fits together

One paragraph, end to end.

A Requirement version owns Commitments, each wrapping an Activity. The kernel — a pure function — takes a Stamp's inputs (requirement version, Baseline ⊕ Excursions, the config core hash, appetites, steering, seed) plus a Profile and the domain MovementModel, and emits the handful of Plans — each plan's identity is its stamp, with materialisation cached and verified. Comparison reads plans' scores under the comparability guard (same stamp basis). Committing writes a SelectionRationale. Execution re-anchors the same kernel to live State (the self-entity's aspects); Observations append to the baseline's fact layer, and the ExecutionLog accumulates the after-action record. Entities are projected across synchronised views and cast to channels or commitments where they must affect the plan. Learning proposes new versions — never mutations.
